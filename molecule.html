How bonding works

Covalent bond

Two atoms share a pair of electrons so both reach a full outer shell. One shared pair is a single bond, two a double bond, three a triple bond. Each atom can only form as many bonds as its valence: H 1, O 2, N 3, C 4.

Polar vs nonpolar

Electronegativity (EN) measures how hard an atom pulls on shared electrons. If the difference ΔEN is below 0.4 the sharing is even (nonpolar). From 0.4 to 1.7 the electrons sit closer to one atom, giving partial charges δ+ and δ− (polar).

Ionic bond

When ΔEN is about 1.7 or more, or a metal meets a nonmetal, one atom takes the electron outright. The result is a positive cation and a negative anion held together by electrostatic attraction, as in NaCl.

Molecular shape (VSEPR)

Bonds and lone pairs are regions of negative charge that repel one another, so they spread as far apart as possible. Lone pairs repel a little more, which squeezes the water angle to about 104.5° instead of 109.5°.
